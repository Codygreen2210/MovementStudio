DESCRIPTION
In 1901, sponge divers pulled a lump of corroded bronze from a Roman-era shipwreck off the Greek island of Antikythera. Inside it were gears: thirty survive, cut by hand, with teeth about 1.6 mm apart. Turn its handle and it showed the Sun and Moon moving through the zodiac, the Moon's phase, a 19-year calendar, the four-year cycle of the Greek games, and the eclipses coming years ahead. Nothing with gearing this complex survives again for well over a thousand years.

We take it apart layer by layer: the crank and the 223-tooth drive wheel, the 64/38 × 48/24 × 127/32 gear train that gives the Moon's 254:19 ratio, the pin-and-slot that makes the Moon speed up and slow down, the Metonic and Saros spirals on the back, and the lost planetary gearing proposed by the 2021 UCL reconstruction.
